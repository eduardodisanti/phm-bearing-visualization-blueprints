Bearing Blueprints

Detecting bearing degradation, and showing the evidence behind it

Four bearings were run to destruction on a single shaft. One reference model of healthy behaviour — trained without ever seeing these units — scored every 10-minute recording. It flagged all four between 14.5 and 74 hours before they failed, and for each alarm it can show which 60 milliseconds of signal caused it.

Scope. Detection is validated: the operational dashboard reproduces results from a manuscript under review. The prognostic view is an exploratory extension — neither manuscript claims or validates a projection of time-to-failure, and with four bearings none could. It is published to show how the trajectory can be modelled and how unstable the resulting dates are, not to predict one.

Research questions

  • Can a user tell normal behaviour from degradation?
  • Can they identify when the degradation began?
  • Can visual evidence explain why a bearing is flagged?
  • Does a failure follow a law, or does it become unpredictable?

Method and reproducibility

A convolutional autoencoder is trained once on simulated healthy signals and transferred unchanged to every unit. The only quantity estimated per bearing is a scalar operating limit, fixed during its first hours of service. Everything shown here is precomputed, so the pages are static and work offline.
