The visualization

Condition-based maintenance asks one thing: what is the state of this asset now, and what should be done about it. That is what this dashboard answers, and it is the project.

Primary · for maintenance engineers

Operational dashboard

Which unit needs attention and since when, when it started deviating, and what the model actually saw inside the signal. Overview to detail in three steps, two controls, no statistics to read.

Try this:
  • Find which bearing was worst at hour 90 — and note that before hour 80 the question has no answer.
  • Open a healthy period, then one near failure.
  • Compare the two waveforms. The scales never move, so the growth you see is real.

Beyond condition monitoring

Estimating remaining useful life is a different question from assessing current condition, and a far harder one. This second view is kept separate and secondary for that reason: it is an exploration of whether the observed degradation can be modelled at all, not a tool for deciding anything.

Secondary · exploratory, not CBM

Prognostic exploration

Which growth law each failure follows, how early a projection can be trusted, and what deciding early costs in accuracy. It also reproduces the published detection results, collapsed at the bottom.

Open the exploration
Scope. Detection is validated: the operational dashboard reproduces results from a manuscript under review. The prognostic view is an exploratory extension — neither manuscript claims or validates a projection of time-to-failure, and with four bearings none could. It is published to show how the trajectory can be modelled and how unstable the resulting dates are, not to predict one.

Research questions

The first three are the ones the project stands on. The fourth is the secondary view's question.

  • Can a user tell normal behaviour from degradation?
  • Can they identify when the degradation began?
  • Can visual evidence explain why a bearing is flagged?
  • Does a failure follow a law, or does it become unpredictable?

The dataset in four numbers

Metric / Value Description
4 bearings, run to failure on one shaft
984 recordings, one every 10 minutes
20 kHz sampling, 20,480 samples per recording
14.5–74 h warning before the documented end of run

Method and reproducibility

A convolutional autoencoder is trained once on simulated healthy signals and transferred unchanged to every unit. The only quantity estimated per bearing is a scalar operating limit, fixed during its first hours of service. Everything shown here is precomputed, so the pages are static and work offline.
